Real Estate Agents: How to Send Listings by Text in Seconds

A buyer sees a sign or an online listing, and texts you: “Is it still available? How much?” In that moment, the agent who replies first with photos, price and the key details is the one who books the showing. Here is a simple system to be that agent.

Real estate is one of the businesses where speed and presentation matter most in a chat. Buyers rarely contact just one agent, and a home is a visual product: nobody gets excited by “3 bed, 2 bath, call me for details”. What moves a buyer to schedule a visit is seeing the home and understanding, at a glance, whether it fits.

The problem is that agents juggle dozens of listings, each with its own photos, price, address and features, scattered across the MLS, the camera roll, email and old conversations. Every inquiry turns into a search. This guide shows how to turn that search into a single tap.

Build a “listing card” for every property

A listing card is a short, standard message you can send for any property. It should be scannable in five seconds on a phone screen:

Listing card

[Street name], [neighborhood]
[Price]
[Beds] bed · [Baths] bath · [Square feet] sq ft
[Garage / yard / pool / standout feature]
[Link to full listing]

Always send it with the best one to three photos: the front of the house, the main living area and the strongest feature (a kitchen, a view, a backyard). More photos belong in the full listing link. In the chat, less is more.

Tip: Keep the order of details identical across listings. Buyers comparing homes will thank you, and so will you when you're writing them.

Organize listings the way you get asked about them

Buyers don't ask for “listing #4521”. They ask for “the one on Oak Street”, “something under 500 with a yard”, or “the condo you posted yesterday”. Organize your listing cards in folders that match how people ask:

  • By status: active, under contract, coming soon.
  • By area: neighborhood or school district.
  • By type: single-family, condo, rental, land.

When a listing sells, move it out of “active” the same day. Sending a buyer a home that's already under contract is one of the quickest ways to lose their trust.

The 60-second first reply

When a new inquiry arrives, aim to send three things within about a minute:

  1. A human greeting with the property name, so they know you read their message.
  2. The listing card with photos.
  3. One question that moves things forward, usually a choice of two showing times.

Example

Hi Jessica! Yes, the house on Maple Ave is still available. Here are the photos and details. I can show it to you Thursday at 5:30 or Saturday at 11. Which works better?

Then, when they reply, ask the qualifying questions: are they pre-approved, what's their timeline, do they have a home to sell. Asking these first, before showing the home, slows down the very moment when the buyer is most interested.

Always have “similar homes” ready

Often the home a buyer asks about isn't the right fit, or it's gone. That's not the end of the conversation; it's your chance to show your value as an agent. Keep two or three alternatives ready for each price range and area, so you can reply:

Example

That one just went under contract, but I have two similar homes nearby in the same price range. Want to see them?

Track every buyer's stage

An agent can easily have dozens of active conversations. Without a simple tracking habit, buyers who said “let me talk to my partner” are never contacted again. Use a short list of stages, for example:

  • New inquiry
  • Showing scheduled
  • Visited
  • Making an offer
  • Closed

Add one note to each buyer after every conversation (budget, must-haves, deal-breakers). The next time a matching home comes up, you'll know exactly who to text first.

Confirm showings in writing

A confirmation message with the date, time and address, plus a reminder the day before, reduces no-shows and looks professional. Save both as templates so they take seconds to send. Our sales message templates include ready-made versions.

Checklist for agents

  • One standard listing card for every active property.
  • The best one to three photos for each listing, ready to send.
  • Listings organized by status, area and type, and updated the day they change.
  • A 60-second first reply with photos and two showing times.
  • Two or three similar homes ready for each price range.
  • Every buyer tracked by stage, with one note per conversation.
  • Saved confirmation and reminder messages for showings.
